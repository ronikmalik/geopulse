# Source registry

The prioritized, verified source table requested against the platform brief. Every row
marked **integrated** has actually been called successfully by this codebase (not just
read about) — see the verification note per row. Every row marked **candidate** has
either been evaluated and found unworkable right now, or is a genuine next-phase
target not yet attempted. `src/lib/sourceRegistry.ts` carries the same licensing fields
in code for the integrated rows; this file is the fuller picture including what didn't
make it in and why.

Fields follow the brief's requested schema: provider, endpoint, auth, rate limit,
license/commercial-use/redistribution, refresh interval, adapter path, and known
limitations.

**Net finding on "live financial updates by region"**, after two vetting passes covering 13 providers total (Alpaca, Alpha Vantage, Finnhub, Marketstack, Financial Modeling Prep, Polygon.io, then Stooq, Yahoo Finance, Twelve Data, EODHD, Tiingo, Trading Economics, Nasdaq Data Link): none close the gap. The pattern is structural, not a search failure — every free tier in this space picks at most two of the three things GeoPulse actually needs (genuinely free at a pollable rate limit, real-time-enough, and legally clean for a public commercial product), and Yahoo's unofficial endpoint proves the *technical* problem is trivially solvable — the blocker is entirely legal/licensing, an explicit ToS prohibition rather than an unwritten gap. The honest state of the art stays what's already integrated: Frankfurter/community-CDN for FX (works well, no caveats) and Finnhub for a single US equity index (US-only on the free plan, confirmed above). Closing the non-US gap for real would require either a paid Finnhub upgrade, a paid plan from one of the providers above, or a direct licensing conversation with Yahoo/a similar provider — not something achievable on a free tier as of this check.

**2026-09-08 cheapest-paid-plan pass**, following the user's follow-up ask ("cheapest paid plan that gives us what we want"): re-checked every provider's actual paid pricing pages for a self-service tier hitting all four of (a) real-time/near-real-time, (b) genuine non-US index coverage, (c) legally clean public commercial display at a *listed* price (not "contact sales"), (d) a normal monthly plan. **None exists.** Ranked by how close each gets:

1. **Finnhub paid tier — closest, ~$50-100/mo (third-party-estimated; Finnhub's real pricing page is JS-rendered and couldn't be directly confirmed).** The only provider whose paid tier explicitly claims added "international stocks" coverage. Caveat that keeps it from being a clean recommendation: Finnhub's FAQ requires commercial users to get sales's written approval regardless of paid plan — so the real total cost (plan price + whatever sales quotes for commercial approval) isn't fully knowable from public pages. **This is the recommended one to actually inquire about** — email sales@finnhub.io, confirm the real international-coverage list and the commercial-approval cost, before paying for anything.
2. **Marketstack Professional, $49.99/mo — cleanest self-service commercial license of any provider (no sales contact needed), but does NOT solve the actual problem**: real-time/intraday stays US-only (IEX) at every tier including the $149.99/mo Business plan; non-US exchanges only ever get end-of-day data no matter how much is paid. Worth knowing about only as a commercially-clean *replacement* for Finnhub's US row, not as an international-coverage fix.
3. Every other provider checked (Alpha Vantage, Twelve Data, EODHD, Financial Modeling Prep, Polygon.io/Massive, Tiingo) fails at least one requirement at every price point up to and including their most expensive listed tier — either real-time international coverage is never offered at any price (Alpha Vantage, Polygon.io), real commercial licensing has no listed price regardless of plan (EODHD, Financial Modeling Prep, Tiingo), or the only tier that's actually commercial-clean costs far more than this project's scale justifies (Twelve Data's Business tier starts at $499/mo and *still* needs per-exchange approval for non-US data).

**Overall recommendation**: there is no clean "pay $X, get international real-time, ship it" answer in this market at small-project scale. If closing this gap is worth pursuing at all, the next real step is a direct email to Finnhub sales confirming (a) the actual current paid-tier price and country list and (b) what, if anything, commercial written approval costs beyond the listed plan price — not a self-service signup.

- Cloudflare Radar — needs an API token (free tier). Would meaningfully improve on
  IODA's noisier outage-anomaly signal with named, categorized outage events.
- RIPE Atlas/RIPEstat — independent BGP/DNS/reachability confirmation layer.

**Cyber & Technology (currently the other zero-country-coverage pillar)**
- No clear path to *country-attributed* cyber risk from a free source identified yet.
  CISA KEV (already integrated) is deliberately a global ticker, not a scored event,
  precisely because it has no country dimension to attribute honestly.

**Sanctions**
- OFAC, EU, UK, UN, OpenSanctions — not started. Would feed the Political & Governance
  and Geopolitical & Security pillars (new/lifted sanctions as discrete events).

## Deduplication policy

The brief is explicit: repeated news articles about the same event are not independent
confirmations. Dedup today is URL-uniqueness (`events.url` is a hard unique
constraint) plus a 24h recency filter, plus real cross-outlet near-duplicate merging
(`src/lib/eventDedup.ts`, title/summary Jaccard similarity) — same-story reports
collapse into one feed card with a source-diversity confidence tier
(`src/lib/correlation.ts`: single-source/corroborated/cross-confirmed). Broader
clustering of genuinely *distinct-but-related* events (GDELT + a wire headline + a
satellite signal about a wider unfolding situation, not the same single incident) is
not implemented — see `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`'s Gap analysis, §1.
